# Photo Brief — hand this to your photographer

Everything needed to shoot and deliver photos that work on this website.
**Print it. Give it to them before the event.**

---

## The one-paragraph version

I need photos that work as **wide banners on a desktop** and **tall crops on a
phone** — so shoot loose, leave room around the subject, and give me the
full-resolution originals. Faces and a full dancefloor are what sell. Empty
floors, backs of heads and blown-out windows are unusable, however technically
good the shot is.

---

## Shot list

### Must-haves

| # | Shot | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Full dancefloor, wide, from above or the side** | The single most valuable image. Proves the promise. |
| 2 | **Schuggie calling, mid-gesture, mic in hand** | Puts a person at the centre of the business |
| 3 | **A couple mid-dance, laughing** | The emotional shot |
| 4 | **Mixed ages in the same frame** — kids and grandparents dancing | Kills the "will everyone join in?" objection |
| 5 | **A wide of the room, dancing, lights on** | For venue/space pages |
| 6 | **Guests watching and grinning from the edge** | Warmth without emptiness |

### Strong extras

- A ceilidh first dance — just the couple, floor cleared
- The band or DJ setup, tidy and professional
- Hands joined in a circle — a detail shot that isn't a face
- Outdoors: marquee or garden, dancing in daylight
- A quaich moment or other Scottish detail
- Corporate: well-dressed adults genuinely enjoying it, not posed

### Never useful

- ❌ An empty dancefloor
- ❌ Backs of heads with no faces
- ❌ Blown-out windows behind the subject
- ❌ Anyone caught mid-blink or mid-chew
- ❌ Heavy filters, crushed blacks, "moody" grading
- ❌ Anything where the subject is smaller than about 1/5 of the frame

## Technical requirements

| | |
|---|---|
| **Format** | RAW if possible, otherwise highest-quality JPEG |
| **Minimum size** | **3000px on the long edge.** Non-negotiable for hero shots. |
| **Aspect** | Shoot both wide and vertical of the key moments |
| **Colour** | Natural. Warm is fine. No heavy grading — the site adds its own tint |
| **Delivery** | Original files, no resizing, no watermark, straight into `_SH_data_in/` |

### Why 3000px

A hero image on a modern phone is displayed at up to ~1200 real pixels wide, at
triple density. Anything under 2000px starts looking soft, and soft photos make
an expensive-looking website look cheap instantly. **Bigger is always fine.
Smaller cannot be rescued.**

---

## Composition — the important bit

Hero photos have text laid over them. That changes how they need to be shot.

### Leave room for words

- **Desktop:** the headline sits on the **left**. Keep your subject **right of
  centre**, and leave the left third relatively uncluttered.
- **Phone:** the text sits at the **bottom**. Keep the subject in the **upper
  two-thirds**.

### Shoot loose

The same photo gets cropped square for phones and wide for desktops. **Leave
generous space around the subject** so both crops work. A perfectly-framed shot
with no breathing room can only ever be used one way.

### The square-crop test

Before you move on, imagine the frame cropped to a square. Is the subject still
the subject? If a square crop loses the people, it can't be a hero.

## Usage

Photos will be used on the website, in social posts and in printed material.
Please confirm licensing covers all three, and let me know how you'd like to be
credited.

## What happens after delivery

*(For whoever prepares the files — not the photographer.)*

1. Originals land in `_SH_data_in/`. They stay there, untouched.
2. Crop to the shape needed — see `_examples/05-images-and-frames.html`.
3. Export WebP (plus JPEG for heroes) at these sizes:

| Use | Export at |
|---|---|
| Hero, desktop | 1800px **and** 2560px wide |
| Hero, phone | 1200 × 1200 square |
| Card | 1200 × 900 |
| Tall frame | 1200 × 1500 |
| Wide frame | 1600 × 1000 |

4. Name for content: `wedding-first-dance.webp`, never `DSC_0012.webp`.
5. Into `assets/images/`, then follow `_howto/04-swap-a-photo.md`.

---

## Consent

Get it before publishing recognisable faces — especially children. A verbal yes
from the couple covers their own photos; guests are their own question. When in
doubt, use a shot where faces are less identifiable, or ask.

This matters more than any of the technical requirements above.
