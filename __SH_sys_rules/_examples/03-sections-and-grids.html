Sections & grids

How a page is built: bands down the screen, with content in columns inside them.

The basic shape of every band

Two layers, always. A section is the full-width stripe; a container keeps the content from running edge to edge.

<section class="section">
  <div class="container">
    …your content…
  </div>
</section>
Never skip the container. Without it, text runs the full width of a desktop screen and becomes unreadable.

Section backgrounds

Alternate them down a page — light, then something stronger, then light again. Never wall-to-wall colour.

.section

The default. Plain ivory.

.section--sage

Soft green. For friendly, reassuring content.

.section--paper2

A subtle shift from the default.

.section--ink

Dark. Use sparingly — short and purposeful.

<section class="section">              <!-- plain ivory -->
<section class="section section--sage">    <!-- soft green -->
<section class="section section--paper2">  <!-- subtle shift -->
<section class="section section--ink">     <!-- dark band -->
<section class="section section--closing"> <!-- final CTA -->

Narrower containers, for reading

Long text needs short lines. Swap the container:

<div class="container container--reading">  <!-- blog posts, guides -->
<div class="container container--narrow">   <!-- a bit narrower than default -->
<div class="container container--tight">    <!-- short centred statements -->

What is reveal?

Adding class="reveal" makes a block fade gently into view as the visitor scrolls to it. Put it on the big blocks — a column, a card, a section intro — not on every paragraph.
