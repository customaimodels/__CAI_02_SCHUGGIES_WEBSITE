Heroes

The block at the top of a page. It has about three seconds to say what this is and why it matters.

What a hero must do

  1. Say who it's for — the small label at the top.
  2. Make one promise — the headline.
  3. Back it up — one or two sentences.
  4. Offer a next step — one obvious button, plus up to three others.

Why five image filenames?

Because a phone and a desktop need different pictures. The picture block hands out:

  • a square crop to phones (a wide photo in a tall phone screen crops the people out of it),
  • a wide one to desktops, in two sizes so big screens get the sharp one and small laptops don't waste data,
  • a JPEG fallback for older browsers.

When you swap a hero photo, change all five. Miss one and some visitors get the old picture. Full instructions in _howto/04-swap-a-photo.md.

Never make the hero taller. On phones it's deliberately capped at about 62% of screen height. Any taller and the photo gets stretched beyond its real size and starts looking blocky. This is the single most common way to make the site look cheap.

The two hero sizes

ClassUse for
heroThe home page. Full height, maximum impact.
hero hero--shortEvery other page. Shorter, gets to the content faster.

grain adds a subtle texture over the photo. Keep it — it's part of the look, and it helps white text stay readable.

Writing the headline

Instead ofWrite
"Welcome to Schuggies-Ceilidhs""The perfect mix of fun, engaging & classy"
"Our Services""Fill your dancefloor from the get-go"
"Party Entertainment""The party everyone actually joins in with"

A headline is a promise, not a label. And keep it to one or two lines on a phone — check before you settle on it.
