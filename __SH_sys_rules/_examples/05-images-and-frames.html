Images & frames

Four shapes. Pick one, crop your photo to match, done.

Crop the photo to fit the frame — never the other way round. Putting a wide photo in a tall frame doesn't stretch it, but it does chop the sides off, and that's usually where the people are.

The four shapes

Tall — 4:5

Best beside a block of text. Export at 1200 × 1500.

The four things every image needs

<img src="../assets/images/photo.webp"   <!-- 1. where the file is -->
     width="1200" height="1500"          <!-- 2. its real size -->
     alt="What is happening in the photo" <!-- 3. a description -->
     loading="lazy">                     <!-- 4. unless it's a hero -->
WhatWhy it matters
srcThe path. Count your ../ — one from pages/, two from pages/blog/.
width / heightReserves the space so the page doesn't jump around as photos load. Use the file's real pixel size.
altWhat a blind visitor hears and Google reads. Describe the scene, not the file.
loading="lazy"Don't load it until the visitor scrolls near it. Never on a hero — that would slow the first view.

Writing alt text

WeakGood
"wedding""A bride throwing both arms in the air mid-celebration, guests behind her"
"IMG_4471""A barn wedding under bunting, Schuggie calling as the floor fills"
"ceilidh photo""Dancers mid-spin in a village hall, a kilted guest laughing"

If the photo is purely decorative, use alt="" — empty, but present.

Before you add any photo

  1. Start from the biggest original in _SH_data_in/.
  2. Crop it to the frame shape you're using.
  3. Export as WebP at the size in the table above.
  4. Name it for what it shows — wedding-first-dance.webp, not DSC_0012.webp.
  5. Put it in assets/images/.
